Alternatives

Folkbranch compared with other family tree software

Looking for an alternative to Ancestry, MyHeritage, Gramps Web or webtrees? They are good at different things, so the honest answer depends on what you want from a family tree. Folkbranch is for a family that keeps its own archive on its own computer or server. This page says where it fits and where it does not.

The short answer

  • Choose Gramps Web if you use Gramps Desktop, need DNA tools or many kinds of reports, or want someone to host it for you.
  • Choose webtrees if you publish your research for others and need fine access rules, several trees, modules or many languages.
  • Choose Folkbranch if the archive is for the family: it runs on a small machine in one command, relatives join from their phones without passwords, and an AI only proposes changes for you to accept.
  • Use Ancestry, MyHeritage or FamilySearch when you need to search the world’s records. Folkbranch has no record collections and no DNA.

Folkbranch next to Gramps Web and webtrees

Gramps Web and webtrees are mature open-source projects with more tools for research. Like Folkbranch, you run them yourself. The differences are in how a family uses them day to day.

Folkbranch and the hosted services

Ancestry, MyHeritage and FamilySearch hold billions of records, DNA tests and other people’s trees. Folkbranch has none of that. They also do more for families than is often thought:

  • Without an account. Ancestry’s “Ask someone for info” lets anyone send facts or photos by a link, for the owner to accept.
  • Voice. MyHeritage MyStories records in the browser and writes the words down. FamilySearch records up to five minutes in the browser, Ancestry in its app.
  • Suggestions. The big services suggest relatives and records for you to accept.

What differs is where the archive lives and who decides. A cloud service keeps your tree on its servers, under its terms. With Folkbranch it is a SQLite file and a folder of photos on your own computer or server, relatives join without an account with a company, faces and speech are handled on the device, and any agent you connect only proposes. Folkbranch itself is free under AGPL-3.0, with no paid plan; you pay only for a server if you rent one. Some hosted services are free only in part, so check their own pricing pages.

The two go together: research on a big service, export GEDCOM, and keep the family’s archive here. See how to bring a tree in and take it out.

What Folkbranch does not do

On purpose, because it needs what a family server does not have:

  • No record collections or hints. They take billions of scanned records and licences.
  • No DNA. It needs a lab test and holds the most sensitive data there is.
  • No world tree and no matching with strangers. The archive is closed to search engines and to strangers by design.
  • No hosted version. You run it yourself, at home or on a server. The install guide shows how, and an AI can do it for you.

Not yet: reports beyond the chart and a person’s page in PDF (a family book is on the roadmap), more than one tree per install, sync with desktop programs, and plugins.

If privacy decides it for you, read how Folkbranch keeps the living private before you choose.

Questions

Is Folkbranch a free alternative to Ancestry or MyHeritage?

Folkbranch is free, open-source family tree software (AGPL-3.0) for keeping your own archive on your own computer or server. It does not replace the record collections or DNA tests of the big services: it has neither. Many people research on a big service, export GEDCOM and keep the family’s archive in Folkbranch.

Folkbranch or Gramps Web?

Choose Gramps Web if you use Gramps Desktop, need DNA tools or many kinds of reports, or want someone to host it for you. Choose Folkbranch if the archive is for the family: it runs on a small machine in one command and relatives join from their phones without passwords.

Folkbranch or webtrees?

Choose webtrees if you publish your research for others and need fine access rules, several trees, modules or many languages. Choose Folkbranch if you want one private archive that relatives can add stories to by link.

Can I move my tree between these programs?

Yes, if the program exports GEDCOM. Folkbranch reads GEDCOM 5.5.1 and 7.0, and Gramps files, and writes GEDCOM 5.5.1, so you can also leave again.
